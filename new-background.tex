\subsection{Detector Retraining and Adaptation}

When participants retain their training data and can run another
optimization process, federated learning coordinates parameter updates while
keeping raw records at their owners
~\cite{federated_learning,Fedprox,ditto2021}. Within intrusion detection,
federated methods adapt this process to non-IID traffic and heterogeneous
local learners~\cite{pourahmadi2023fedoe,xu2026entente}. Beyond federated
learning, recent NIDS methods also revise the training pipeline under difficult
data conditions. For example, RAPIER handles low quality labels for encrypted
malicious traffic~\cite{qing2024rapier}, Rosetta uses TCP aware augmentation to
improve robustness across network environments~\cite{xie2023rosetta}, and CoLD
denoises labels before fitting a classifier~\cite{yang2026cold}. Unlike methods
that revise training, our setting begins after the contributed detectors have
been trained and their historical records are no longer available.

\subsection{Pretrained Model Integration}

When source records disappear but their trained models remain accessible,
pretrained-model integration provides the closest starting point. In this
setting, prior approaches either rank a heterogeneous model zoo by cross-domain
transferability and aggregate its top $K$ models~\cite{dong2022zood} or combine
pretrained models without accessing their source records
~\cite{wei2024tgr,zhao2024modelglue}. They do not select one fixed detector
subset under a hard aggregate budget while treating class support as a strict
priority.

\subsection{Output-Level Ensembles and Cost-Aware Selection}

Once model outputs are available, stacked generalization can train a
downstream classifier over them~\cite{wolpert1992stacked}. In security, SIRAJ
aggregates reports from heterogeneous threat intelligence sources by
pretraining an embedding and fine tuning downstream predictors
~\cite{thirumuruganathan2022siraj}, while Fed-MStacking trains a global
meta-classifier from heterogeneous local predictions in a missing-label
setting~\cite{qiu2024fedmstacking}. To account for cost during inference,
SPIREL learns a policy for each item that trades off model invocations and
classification utility~\cite{birman2022spirel}. Although these approaches
combine outputs or control the cost of each item, they neither screen detectors
nor return one fixed subset under a hard aggregate budget. Before fitting one
shared classifier over the selected outputs, \Fname makes this subset decision.

\subsection{Local Privacy for Prediction Uploads}

Because prediction outputs can reveal information about their inputs, a
privacy guarantee must identify both where randomization occurs and which
fields it covers. For example, PrivateKT perturbs locally produced predictions
before sending them for knowledge transfer~\cite{qi2023privatekt}, whereas
differentially private federated trees protect statistics exchanged while
training a new model~\cite{dpfedtree_ccs22}. Because \Fname uploads selected detector
scores to fit a shared classifier while treating labels as public, we
specialize the local model of Duchi et al.~\cite{duchi2013localprivacy} to this
release.

{
\begin{definition}[Label-conditional local differential privacy]
\label{def:local_dp}
For a fixed public label $y$, a client applies a randomized mechanism
$\mathcal M_y$ to the feature vector $x$. The upload
$\mathcal R(x,y)=(\mathcal M_y(x),y)$ satisfies label-conditional
$(\epsilon,0)$-local differential privacy if, for every fixed $y$, every pair
$x,x'$, and every measurable output set $\mathcal O$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\mathcal M_y(x)\in\mathcal O]
\le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal M_y(x')\in\mathcal O].
\label{eq:local_dp_definition}
\end{equation}
The definition compares records with the same public label and protects the
randomized, feature-derived output.
\end{definition}
}

To instantiate Definition~\ref{def:local_dp}, let a deterministic score
map $g$ have replacement sensitivity
\begin{equation}
\Delta_1(g)=\sup_{x,x'}\lVert g(x)-g(x')\rVert_1.
\label{eq:local_l1_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
When independent noise
$\eta_k\sim\operatorname{Lap}(0,\Delta_1(g)/\epsilon)$ is added to every
coordinate, the mechanism satisfies
Definition~\ref{def:local_dp}~\cite{dwork2006calibrating}. If one
record releases blocks with parameters $\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_s$,
sequential composition gives parameter $\sum_{i=1}^{s}\epsilon_i$. Under
one-record replacement, releases derived from unchanged records do not add to
this privacy parameter.

In \Fname, this mechanism applies to the selected detector-score blocks.
Theorem~\ref{thm:local_score_privacy} establishes label-conditional local
privacy for that score upload under the public-label adjacency relation.
