\title{\Fname: Integrating Pretrained Intrusion Detectors under Deployment Budgets}

% Anonymous submission: author names, affiliations, and email addresses are omitted.
\author{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Deployed intrusion detectors may degrade under concept drift, yet continual retraining is often impractical because historical network records are no longer accessible due to privacy and retention constraints.
We therefore study post-training integration of authorized pretrained detectors, aiming to select a detector set under a hard deployment budget while preserving diverse source attack-class knowledge and maintaining predictive quality on current target data.
We present \Fname, which treats source coverage and current predictive behavior as ordered objectives rather than combining them into a single score.
\Fname aligns heterogeneous detector outputs, uses held-out target records to assess current behavior, exactly maximizes weighted source class coverage under a logical byte budget via dynamic programming, and then refines the selected set to improve current prediction without reducing coverage.
A shared stacker integrates the selected outputs, with optional Laplace perturbation for score uploads.
Theoretically, we establish that calibration remains valid after adaptive selection and that the primary coverage problem is solved exactly, while perturbed scores satisfy label-conditional local differential privacy.
Across four cybersecurity datasets with 60+ candidate detectors per task, \Fname reduces communication by approximately half under a 50\% selection-cost budget while preserving maximum weighted source coverage and achieving detection performance comparable to the full detector pool.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Deployed intrusion detection models often suffer from performance degradation under concept drift~\cite{andresini2021insomnia,yang2021cade}, as evolving network behaviors can invalidate the patterns learned during training.
However, continual retraining is challenging because the original network logs used for model training are often inaccessible due to their sensitive nature. Such logs may contain confidential information, including internal network topologies and user identities~\cite{nist2016threatsharing,xu2026entente}, and may need to be deleted to comply with privacy regulations such as the GDPR~\cite{gdpr2018general}.
Therefore, a natural solution is to leverage intrusion detection models from other parties to collaboratively improve detection performance without requiring access to private training data.

Existing solutions include collaborative learning (\ie, federated learning)~\cite{federated_learning,Fedprox,ditto2021,zhao2023fedprompt} and pretrained-model integration. 
However, collaborative retraining requires source training data of clients (\ie, contributor) to remain available and contributors to continue computing and communicating model updates.
In contrast, pretrained model reuse can exploit pretrained source models without retaining their original training records~\cite{old_dogs2019,wei2024tgr}.
Yet existing approaches either select or aggregate pretrained models without explicitly accounting for heterogeneous deployment costs~\cite{dong2022zood,zhao2024modelglue}, or perform cost-aware dynamic detector selection rather than choosing a single fixed detector set for deployment~\cite{birman2022spirel}.
This gap motivates our research question: \emph{How can a coordinator select and integrate a fixed set of pretrained intrusion detectors under a deployment budget, while preserving diverse intrusion knowledge from the source domains without sacrificing competence on the current target domain?}

Answering this question presents three challenges.
First, detectors contributed by different parties may use different label spaces and support different attack classes, making their outputs difficult to compare and integrate directly.
Second, during integration, preserving source attack knowledge and maintaining current detection performance are not always aligned.
A detector that performs well on current data may contribute little new class knowledge, whereas a detector with unique class coverage may no longer perform well after distribution shifts.
Therefore, the coordinator must preserve unique source coverage without filling the limited budget with detectors that are currently weak or redundant.
Third, detector selection must remain reliable and privacy-aware under the deployment budget. Because calibration data are used to choose detectors, their performance estimates must remain valid after selection, while uploaded prediction scores may still reveal sensitive information.


To address these challenges, we present \Fname, a post-training framework for budget-aware integration of frozen intrusion detectors.
The key idea is to prioritize source class coverage over current predictive performance: \Fname first selects a detector set that preserves the maximum weighted source attack knowledge allowed by the deployment budget, and then improves current predictive behavior without reducing this coverage.
This ordering reflects the different roles of the two signals: source class counts indicate which attack classes were represented in the source training data, whereas held-out current records reveal whether the pretrained detectors remain useful after distribution shifts~\cite{yang2021cade}.
To realize this design, \Fname first aligns heterogeneous detector outputs to a common label space and uses calibration data to assess their current behavior. It then exactly maximizes weighted source class coverage under the budget through dynamic programming, refines the selected set without sacrificing coverage, and integrates the selected outputs with a shared stacker~\cite{wolpert1992stacked}.


We further establish theoretical guarantees for calibration and primary coverage optimization.
Because avoiding raw-data sharing alone does not eliminate privacy risks from model outputs~\cite{ico2023aiguidance}, \Fname also supports optional Laplace perturbation before score upload.
The perturbed scores provide record-level, label-conditional local differential privacy, while this guarantee does not extend to public labels or contributed detector parameters.

We evaluate \Fname on four intrusion detection datasets with over 60 candidate detectors per task.
At a 50\% selection cost budget, \Fname maximizes weighted source class coverage while reducing total logical communication by 49.3\% to 50.4\%, demonstrating that diverse source knowledge can be retained at roughly half the communication cost.
Importantly, these savings incur only a small loss in detection quality: across all four datasets, the macro F1 gap from the full detector pool is limited to 0.028.
Consistent with the exactness guarantee, the primary solver attains the same coverage as exhaustive search in all 40 comparison instances.
In summary, we make four contributions.
\begin{packeditemize}
    \item We formalize post-training detector integration under a deployment
budget and separate preserved source class coverage from current predictive
    competence through an ordered objective.
    %
    \item We develop \Fname to align pretrained heterogeneous detectors, solve the
    primary coverage problem exactly, refine current behavior without reducing
    coverage, and train one shared stacker from optionally perturbed scores.
    %
    \item We establish selection valid calibration bounds, exact primary
    optimization, local secondary stability, label conditional privacy for
    randomized score uploads, and a coverage guarantee under bounded
    client mixture shift.
    %
    \item We evaluate \Fname across four cybersecurity tasks and show when a
    50\% selection cost budget provides near full pool quality with
    approximately half the communication, as well as where coverage, detection
    quality, privacy, and runtime impose measurable limits.
\end{packeditemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
We first present and review nearby integration and selection methods, and then
introduce the notion of differential privacy.

\subsection{Detector Retraining and Adaptation}

When participants retain local training data and can rerun optimization,
federated learning coordinates their parameter updates without transferring
raw records~\cite{federated_learning,Fedprox,ditto2021}.
%
Within intrusion detection, federated methods adapt this process to non-IID
traffic and heterogeneous local
learners~\cite{pourahmadi2023fedoe,xu2026entente}.
%
Recent NIDS research also redesigns the training pipeline to withstand adverse
data conditions.
%
For example, RAPIER handles low quality labels for encrypted malicious
traffic~\cite{qing2024rapier}, Rosetta uses TCP aware augmentation to improve
robustness across network environments~\cite{xie2023rosetta}, and CoLD denoises
labels before fitting a classifier~\cite{yang2026cold}.
%
These approaches assume that participants can revisit training, whereas our
setting begins only after the contributed detectors have been trained and their
historical records are no longer available.

\subsection{Pretrained Model Integration}

When source records are no longer available but their trained models remain
accessible, pretrained model integration is the closest line of work.
%
ZooD ranks a heterogeneous model zoo by cross domain transferability and
aggregates its top $K$ models~\cite{dong2022zood}, while other approaches
combine pretrained models without accessing their source
records~\cite{wei2024tgr,zhao2024modelglue}.
%
However, these methods do not solve the constrained selection problem studied
here: choosing one fixed detector subset under a hard aggregate budget while
treating available class support as a strict priority.

\subsection{Output Level Ensembles and Cost Aware Selection}

Once detector outputs are available, stacked generalization can train a
downstream classifier over them~\cite{wolpert1992stacked}.
%
In security, SIRAJ aggregates reports from heterogeneous threat intelligence
sources by pretraining an embedding and finetuning downstream
predictors~\cite{thirumuruganathan2022siraj}, while FedMStacking trains a
global meta classifier from heterogeneous local predictions when labels are
missing~\cite{qiu2024fedmstacking}.
%
To account for inference cost, SPIREL learns a per item policy that trades off
model invocations against classification utility~\cite{birman2022spirel}.
%
Although these approaches combine outputs or control per item cost, none
simultaneously screens detectors on current data and returns one fixed subset
under a hard aggregate budget.
%
By contrast, \Fname makes this subset decision before fitting a shared
classifier over the selected outputs.

\subsection{Local Privacy for Prediction Uploads}

Prediction outputs can reveal information about their inputs, so a privacy
guarantee must specify both where randomization occurs and which fields it
protects.
%
For example, PrivateKT perturbs locally produced predictions before uploading
them for knowledge transfer~\cite{qi2023privatekt}, whereas differentially
private federated trees protect the statistics exchanged while training a new
model~\cite{dpfedtree_ccs22}.
%
Our setting differs because \Fname uploads scores from selected detectors to
fit a shared classifier while treating labels as public.
%
We therefore specialize the local model of Duchi et
al.~\cite{duchi2013localprivacy} to this release.

{
\begin{definition}[Label conditional local differential privacy]
\label{def:local_dp}
For a fixed public label $y$, a client applies a randomized mechanism
$\mathcal M_y$ to the feature vector $x$.
%
The upload
$\mathcal R(x,y)=(\mathcal M_y(x),y)$ satisfies label conditional
$(\epsilon,0)$-local differential privacy if, for every fixed $y$, every pair
$x,x'$, and every measurable output set $\mathcal O$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\mathcal M_y(x)\in\mathcal O]
\le e^{\epsilon}\Pr[\mathcal M_y(x')\in\mathcal O].
\label{eq:local_dp_definition}
\end{equation}
%
This definition compares records with the same public label and protects the
feature derived portion of the upload, and the label itself remains public.
\end{definition}
}

To instantiate Definition~\ref{def:local_dp}, let the deterministic score map
$g$ have replacement sensitivity
\begin{equation}
\Delta_1(g)=\sup_{x,x'}\lVert g(x)-g(x')\rVert_1.
\label{eq:local_l1_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
%
Adding independent noise
$\eta_k\sim\operatorname{Lap}(0,\Delta_1(g)/\epsilon)$ to every coordinate
yields a mechanism that satisfies
Definition~\ref{def:local_dp}~\cite{dwork2006calibrating}.
%
If one record releases blocks with parameters
$\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_s$, sequential composition gives the parameter
$\sum_{i=1}^{s}\epsilon_i$.
%
Under one record replacement, releases derived from unchanged records do not
increase this privacy parameter.

In \Fname, this mechanism applies to the selected detector score blocks.
%
Theorem~\ref{thm:local_score_privacy} establishes label conditional local
privacy for the resulting score upload under the public label adjacency
relation.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Post training integration must select among pretrained detectors even when their
source records are no longer available.
%
We formalize this setting, define the ordered selection objective, and state
the execution and information boundaries used by the design and analysis.

\subsection{System Model}

After local fitting ends, $J$ clients expose a pool of $M$ pretrained candidate
detectors.
%
Each client contributes one candidate in our experiments, although \Fname
allows $J$ and $M$ to vary independently.
%
For candidate $i$, let $p_i(x)\in\mathbb R^{h_i}$ denote its native
probability output.
%
Because candidates may use incompatible label coordinates, the coordinator
validates a hard semantic map
\begin{equation}
A_i\in\{0,1\}^{(C+1)\times h_i},
\qquad
\mathbf 1^\top A_i=\mathbf 1^\top,
\end{equation}
and forms
\begin{equation}
q_i(x)=A_i p_i(x)\in\Delta^{C},
\label{eq:aligned_output}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta^C$ is the probability simplex with $C+1$ coordinates.
%
We align the first $C$ coordinates with a pretrained target ontology and reserve
the last coordinate, denoted by $\bot$, for unsupported outputs.
%
Every later Brier calculation therefore retains, rather than silently
discarding, probability mass assigned to $\bot$.

Before selection, the coordinator holds a labeled calibration set
$\mathcal D^{\rm cal}$ from the current deployment domain.
%
We keep this set disjoint from the records used for candidate fitting, stacker
fitting, and sealed testing.
%
The coordinator also freezes every candidate, semantic map, and source side
class count before evaluating the pool.
%
Test labels remain sealed until both the selected set and the stacker are
fixed, so they cannot affect calibration, cost, or selection.

Selection consumes communication in both model delivery and score upload.
%
To enforce one hard budget across these steps, \Fname assigns each selected
candidate a logical cost.
%
Let $K_i$ be candidate $i$'s serialized size, let $B_t$ be the number of
stacker fit records, and let $d$ be the logical byte width of one probability
element.
%
For a selected set $S$, define
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{sel}}(S)=\sum_{i\in S}b_i,
\qquad
b_i=J K_i+dB_t(C+1).
\label{eq:candidate_cost}
\end{equation}
The first term accounts for delivering a selected candidate to $J$ clients,
whereas the second accounts for uploading one aligned probability block per
stacker fit record.
%
Given a budget fraction $\beta$, we set
\begin{equation}
B=\left\lfloor\beta\sum_{i=1}^{M}b_i\right\rfloor.
\label{eq:logical_budget}
\end{equation}
The denominator includes every candidate, including candidates that may later
fail calibration.
%
For complete logical communication, we additionally count the initial
candidate upload and the stacker label upload:
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{total}}(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}K_i+C_{\mathrm{sel}}(S)+dB_t.
\label{eq:total_logical_communication}
\end{equation}
We use $C_{\mathrm{sel}}$ to enforce the selection budget and
$C_{\mathrm{total}}$ to compare the complete communication of selected and
full pool configurations.

\subsection{Detector Selection Objective}

Alignment makes detector outputs comparable, but it does not determine which
detectors should survive the budget.
%
We next define the ordered objective that governs this choice.

Ranking only by average current utility may discard the sole affordable
candidate that supports an important class.
%
Ranking only by source side support can instead retain candidates whose current
probabilities are weak or redundant.
%
A single weighted sum does not resolve this conflict because enough secondary
gain can compensate for a positive coverage loss.
%
\Fname therefore gives weighted source side coverage strict priority and
compares current behavior only among sets that attain the same primary value.

With that priority fixed, \Fname targets
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{lexmax}_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal R}}
\left(C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S),\Phi(S)\right)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_{i\in S}b_i\le B.
\label{eq:lexicographic_target}
\end{equation}
Here, $\widehat{\mathcal R}$ contains the candidates that pass calibration,
$C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ measures weighted source support, and $\Phi$ summarizes
current predictive scores.
%
The primary solver computes the first coordinate exactly.
%
The deployed refinement then improves the secondary coordinate only through
coverage neutral local operations, and it does not claim a globally optimal
secondary value.
%
Only the optional small pool exhaustive solver computes the complete
lexicographic optimum.

\subsection{Execution and Analysis Model}
\label{sec:information_boundary}

The guarantees depend on which artifacts the coordinator observes and where
randomization occurs.
%
We therefore make the execution and privacy models explicit.

We evaluate the workflow in a centralized simulation, with
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} tracing the protocol's logical data dependencies.
%
In a client side realization, each client perturbs its selected probability
blocks with fresh private Laplace randomness before upload.
%
The optional sensitivity mode samples the same distribution centrally only to
measure its utility effect.

The privacy analysis treats labels as public and protects the feature dependent
information conveyed by the randomized score uploads used to fit the stacker.
%
It assumes immutable candidate detectors, structurally validated semantic
maps, honest source support reports, and fresh client side randomness.
%
The resulting guarantee is label conditional and applies to the score upload,
not to the contributed detector parameters.

\section{Design of \Fname}
\label{sec:approach}

\Fname addresses the three design challenges in sequence: it makes
heterogeneous outputs comparable, selects detectors without relaxing the
primary coverage target, and learns from only the selected outputs.
%
This section follows that sequence from calibration through shared stacking.

\subsection{Selection and Stacking Workflow}

Detector selection and stacker fitting require separate data so that
stacker fit records cannot influence which detectors are chosen.
%
As Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} shows, the coordinator uses only calibration
outputs to determine eligibility and the selected set.
%
After the coordinator freezes that set, clients evaluate the independent
stacker fit records and upload only the selected aligned blocks, optionally
perturbed, together with their labels.
%
Every contributed detector remains pretrained throughout stacking.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:silostitch} summarizes the complete
procedure between client and server, and the following subsections define \textsc{Calibrate},
\textsc{Cover}, and \textsc{Refine} in detail.
%
Here, $\epsilon_b$ is the privacy parameter assigned to one selected detector
block, and $\epsilon_b=\infty$ disables perturbation, so
$\operatorname{Lap}(0,0)$ denotes zero noise.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Fname Procedure}
\label{alg:silostitch}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\{f_i,A_i,H_i,K_i\}_{i=1}^{M}$, $\mathcal D^{\rm cal}$,
$\{\mathcal D_j^{\rm stk}\}_{j=1}^{J}$, $B$, $\epsilon_b\in(0,\infty]$
\Ensure $\widehat S$, $\widehat f$
\State Clients $\to$ Server: $\{f_i,A_i,H_i,K_i\}_{i=1}^{M}$
\State $(\widehat{\mathcal R},\Gamma,w,b,\Phi)\gets
\Call{Calibrate}{\mathcal D^{\rm cal}}$
\State $(W^\star,S_0)\gets\Call{Cover}{\widehat{\mathcal R},\Gamma,w,b,B}$
\State $\widehat S\gets\Call{Refine}{S_0,W^\star,\Phi,B}$
\State Server $\to$ Clients: $\{f_i,A_i\}_{i\in\widehat S}$
\For{$j=1,\ldots,J$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $Z_j\gets\operatorname{concat}_{i\in\widehat S}q_i(X_j^{\rm stk})$
    \State $(\Xi_j)_{t,i,k}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
    \operatorname{Lap}(0,2/\epsilon_b)$
    \State $\widetilde Z_j\gets Z_j+\Xi_j$
    \State Client $j\to$ Server: $(\widetilde Z_j,Y_j^{\rm stk})$
\EndFor
\State $\widehat f\gets\Call{Fit}{\bigcup_j(\widetilde Z_j,Y_j^{\rm stk})}$
\State \Return $(\widehat S,\widehat f)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Probability Alignment and Brier Calibration}

The selector needs comparable, bounded scores from detectors with different
native outputs.
%
We obtain these scores by evaluating the aligned probability vectors with a
full vector Brier score.

For calibration record $(x_t,y_t)$, let $e_{y_t}\in\mathbb R^{C+1}$ be the
one hot label vector with a zero $\bot$ coordinate.
%
We evaluate each candidate with the complete aligned output and define
\begin{equation}
\ell_{i,t}=\frac12\left\|q_i(x_t)-e_{y_t}\right\|_2^2,
\qquad
g_{i,t}=1-\ell_{i,t}\in[0,1].
\label{eq:brier_utility}
\end{equation}
Thus, $g_{i,t}$ is one full vector Brier utility rather than a collection of
coordinate wise scores.
%
Including the $\bot$ coordinate ensures that unsupported probability mass
reduces this utility.

\bheading{Eligibility screening} An empirical mean alone can admit a candidate
whose current risk is too high or too uncertain.
%
Using $N$ calibration records and failure level $\delta\in(0,1)$, we compute
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_i=\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}g_{i,t},
\qquad
r=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\delta)}{2N}},
\label{eq:overall_calibration}
\end{equation}
and admit candidate $i$ only if
\begin{equation}
R_i^+=1-\widehat\mu_i+r\le\tau_L.
\label{eq:risk_gate}
\end{equation}
The gate defines the calibration eligible pool
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal R}=\{i:R_i^+\le\tau_L\}.
\label{eq:eligible_pool}
\end{equation}
Eligibility therefore depends on an upper confidence bound on current risk,
not on empirical utility alone.

\bheading{Classwise scores} Average eligibility does not reveal whether a
candidate remains useful for a particular class.
%
We retain the class-specific scores needed by secondary refinement and the
later coverage certificate.
%
For class $c$, let $N_c=\sum_t\mathbf 1\{y_t=c\}$ and
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_{i,c}=\frac1{N_c}\sum_{t:y_t=c}g_{i,t}.
\end{equation}
Because \Fname requires every calibration class to occur, $N_c>0$.
%
We then compute the class conditional radius and lower utility as
\begin{equation}
r_c=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4MC/\delta)}{2N_c}},
\qquad
a_{i,c}=\left[\widehat\mu_{i,c}-r_c\right]_+,
\label{eq:class_lower_utility}
\end{equation}
where $[z]_+=\max\{0,z\}$.
%
The secondary objective and effective coverage certificate use $a_{i,c}$ as a
conservative class-specific score.

\bheading{Behavioral similarity} Eligibility alone does not prevent two
candidates from behaving almost identically.
%
\Fname measures this redundancy on a deterministically selected,
class stratified anchor set $\mathcal A$ and forms
\begin{equation}
z_i=\operatorname{vec}\left([q_i(x_t)]_{t\in\mathcal A}\right),
\qquad
\kappa_{i,k}=\left[
\frac{\langle z_i,z_k\rangle}{\|z_i\|_2\|z_k\|_2}
\right]_{0}^{1}.
\label{eq:behavior_similarity}
\end{equation}
Here, $[\cdot]_0^1$ clips numerical roundoff into $[0,1]$.
%
We use $\kappa_{i,k}$ in a facility location term that measures
representativeness, not as a pairwise diversity score.

\subsection{Coverage Prioritized Detector Selection}

Calibration measures current behavior, but it cannot preserve the range of
source side class support by itself.
%
We therefore make weighted semantic coverage the primary budgeted objective.

\bheading{Weighted semantic coverage} For candidate $i$ and target class $c$,
let $H_{i,c}$ be the accepted source side support count after semantic mapping.
%
After fixing $n_{\min}>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{i,c}=\mathbf 1\{H_{i,c}\ge n_{\min}\}.
\label{eq:semantic_presence}
\end{equation}
If $H_c=\sum_i H_{i,c}>0$, we define
\begin{equation}
w_c=\frac{H_c^{-1/2}}{\sum_{d=1}^{C}H_d^{-1/2}},
\qquad
\sum_{c=1}^{C}w_c=1.
\label{eq:semantic_weights}
\end{equation}
Classes with less aggregate source support consequently receive greater
weight.
%
For any selected set $S$,
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S)=
\sum_{c=1}^{C}w_c
\mathbf 1\{\exists i\in S:\Gamma_{i,c}=1\}.
\label{eq:semantic_coverage}
\end{equation}
Because the $\bot$ coordinate represents unsupported outputs, we exclude it
from this coverage.
%
Accordingly, $C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ measures the range of declared source
support rather than current predictive accuracy.

\bheading{Secondary behavioral objective} Once the primary coverage value is
fixed, current scores can distinguish among coverage equivalent sets.
%
With $h_\tau(z)=1-e^{-z/\tau}$, define
\begin{align}
U(S)
&=\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}
h_{\tau_U}\!\left(\sum_{i\in S}g_{i,t}\right),
\nonumber\\
C_{\mathrm{pred}}(S)
&=\frac1C\sum_{c=1}^{C}
h_{\tau_C}\!\left(\sum_{i\in S}a_{i,c}\right),
\nonumber\\
D_{\mathrm{rep}}(S)
&=\frac1{|\widehat{\mathcal R}|}
\sum_{k\in\widehat{\mathcal R}}
\max_{i\in S}\kappa_{i,k}.
\label{eq:secondary_components}
\end{align}
The term $U$ summarizes record level utility, $C_{\mathrm{pred}}$ summarizes
conservative classwise scores, and $D_{\mathrm{rep}}$ rewards behavior that
represents the eligible pool.
%
The saturating functions impose diminishing returns so that repeated scores
cannot dominate the secondary comparison without bound.
%
Here and throughout the analysis, we set
$\max_{i\in\varnothing}\kappa_{i,k}=0$.
%
We average the classes uniformly in $C_{\mathrm{pred}}$, whereas
$C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ uses source support weights.
%
For nonnegative weights that sum to one, the secondary objective is
\begin{equation}
\Phi(S)=\lambda_U U(S)+\lambda_C C_{\mathrm{pred}}(S)
+\lambda_D D_{\mathrm{rep}}(S).
\label{eq:secondary_objective}
\end{equation}
\Fname uses this score only after fixing semantic coverage, so no increase in
$\Phi$ can compensate for a decrease in $C_{\mathrm{sem}}$.

\bheading{Budgeted selection procedure} The hard budget induces the feasible
family and exact primary target
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_B&=\left\{
\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal R}:
\sum_{i\in S}b_i\le B
\right\},\\
W^\star&=\max_{S\in\mathcal F_B}C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:primary_value}
\end{equation}
We encode every $\Gamma_i$ as a $C$-bit mask and use a sparse dynamic program
to compute $W^\star$ and recover a least cost witness.
%
Starting from this exact primary witness, we spend remaining budget through
density-based fill, accepting the chosen candidate only while its absolute gain
exceeds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loc}}$.
%
We then accept a single swap replacement only when it preserves $W^\star$ and
improves $\Phi$ by more than the same fixed tolerance.
%
These operations refine the secondary objective locally without weakening the
exact primary guarantee.

For a matched comparison, we also apply the same fill--one swap procedure
without semantic priority.
%
If that no semantic result already attains $W^\star$, \Fname returns the same
set unchanged.
%
Otherwise, it refines the exact coverage witness and, when available, a
coverage optimal singleton seed, then returns the locally refined result with
the larger $\Phi$ value.
%
The two variants thus coincide when the semantic constraint is inactive and
differ only when semantic priority changes the refinement path.

\subsection{Shared Stacker Learning}

After the selector freezes $\widehat S$, only the selected aligned outputs
enter the shared representation:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\widehat S}(x)=
\operatorname{concat}_{i\in\widehat S}q_i(x)
\in\mathbb R^{|\widehat S|(C+1)}.
\label{eq:selected_representation}
\end{equation}

\bheading{Local score perturbation} Selected score blocks may reveal
record level information even though source records are not shared.
%
When local score protection is enabled, client $j$ independently perturbs
every coordinate of each selected block:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde q_i(x_t)=q_i(x_t)+\eta_{i,t},
\qquad
\eta_{i,t,k}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
\operatorname{Lap}\!\left(0,\frac{2}{\epsilon_b}\right).
\label{eq:local_laplace_release}
\end{equation}
The resulting perturbed representation is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde\phi_{\widehat S}(x)=
\operatorname{concat}_{i\in\widehat S}\widetilde q_i(x).
\label{eq:private_selected_representation}
\end{equation}

\bheading{Shared stacker fitting} The independent stacker fit partition
$\mathcal D^{\mathrm{stk}}$ supplies the labels and selected score features
used to train
\begin{equation}
\widehat f_{\widehat S}=\mathsf{Fit}_{\mathrm{global}}
\left(
\{(\widetilde\phi_{\widehat S}(x_t),y_t):
(x_t,y_t)\in\mathcal D^{\mathrm{stk}}\}
\right).
\label{eq:shared_stacker}
\end{equation}
For the nonprivate configuration, we set
$\widetilde\phi_{\widehat S}=\phi_{\widehat S}$.
%
Both configurations use only the columns indexed by $\widehat S$, so
unselected blocks cannot affect the learned classifier.

The centralized sensitivity experiment samples from
Eq.~\ref{eq:local_laplace_release} after selection to isolate the utility
effect of noise.
%
In a distributed realization, each client samples the same noise privately.
%
During sealed test inference, \Fname preserves the selected layout and leaves
every contributed detector unchanged.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:method_analysis}

The analysis follows the guarantee chain created by the design: privacy holds
at score upload, calibration remains valid under adaptive selection, the
primary solver is exact, and the secondary refinement has a local certificate.
%
We then bound how client mixture shift changes the interpretation of the
pretrained set's current scores.

\subsection{Local Privacy of Score Uploads}

Because the coordinator receives selected score blocks, the first question is
what the client side randomization protects.
%
We answer it under the execution boundary defined in
Section~\ref{sec:information_boundary}.

We fix the selected set before observing any stacker fit record and treat each
record label as public.
%
Accordingly, $(x,y)$ and $(x',y)$ are adjacent only when their feature vectors
differ.
%
Because Algorithm~\ref{alg:silostitch} uploads $y$ without perturbation, we use
this label fixed relation.
%
For a batched upload, adjacent batches have the same size, row alignment, and
label sequence, and they differ in exactly one feature vector.
%
Each client samples noise independently across records, selected detector
blocks, and output coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Label conditional local score privacy]
\label{thm:local_score_privacy}
Suppose that the selected detectors and their semantic maps are fixed and
that $0<\epsilon_b<\infty$ and each client uses fresh private randomness in
Eq.~\ref{eq:local_laplace_release}.
%
Then one released detector block is
$(\epsilon_b,0)$-locally differentially private under the preceding
adjacency relation.
%
If $s=|\widehat S|$ blocks are released for the same
record, their joint release is $(s\epsilon_b,0)$-locally differentially
private.
%
Under one record replacement, releasing the entire client batch has
the same record level guarantee.
%
More generally, assigning parameter
$\epsilon_i$ to block $i$ gives
$(\sum_{i\in\widehat S}\epsilon_i,0)$-local differential privacy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $q_i(x)$ and $q_i(x')$ are probability vectors,
$\lVert q_i(x)-q_i(x')\rVert_1\le2$.
%
Therefore, coordinatewise Laplace noise
with scale $2/\epsilon_b$ bounds the density ratio of one released block by
$e^{\epsilon_b}$.
%
Sequential composition over the $s$ selected blocks gives
$e^{s\epsilon_b}$.
%
Because different rows use independent randomization and
neighboring batches differ in one row, parallel composition across rows leaves
this bound unchanged.
%
Appending the unchanged public labels does not alter the
density ratio.
\end{proof}

Assigning $\epsilon_b=\epsilon/s$ to each block changes the noise scale to
$2s/\epsilon$ and yields a total per record score budget $\epsilon$.
%
For a fixed total budget, selecting fewer blocks also reduces the noise scale
required by this composition rule.
%
This guarantee is label conditional and covers the randomized score upload.
%
Privacy parameters compose across repeated upload rounds.

\subsection{Calibration under Heterogeneous Clients}

The privacy result concerns the stacker fit partition, whereas detector
eligibility depends on a separate calibration partition.
%
We next show that the calibration scores remain valid after the coordinator
uses it to select a detector set.

The records in $\mathcal D^{\rm cal}$ may represent different clients and
classes, so an identically distributed population model would obscure the
mixture being certified.
%
We condition on both the observed client--label design
$\mathcal I=\{(j_t,y_t)\}_{t=1}^{N}$ and the candidate pool $\mathcal Q$
pretrained before calibration.
%
We assume conditional independence given $(\mathcal Q,\mathcal I)$ while
allowing the conditional distribution to differ across records.
%
Define
\begin{equation}
\mu_i=\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}\mathbb E[g_{i,t}\mid\mathcal Q,\mathcal I],
\qquad
R_i=1-\mu_i,
\label{eq:heterogeneous_overall_mean}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mu_{i,c}=\frac1{N_c}\sum_{t:y_t=c}
\mathbb E[g_{i,t}\mid\mathcal Q,\mathcal I].
\label{eq:heterogeneous_class_mean}
\end{equation}
These means describe the record weighted calibration mixture, not equal client
or worst client performance.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous calibration]
\label{thm:simultaneous_calibration}
Under the preceding assumptions, with probability at least $1-\delta$, the
following inequalities hold simultaneously for every candidate $i$ and
class $c$:
\begin{align}
R_i&\le R_i^+\le R_i+2r,
\label{eq:simultaneous_risk}\\
\max\{0,\mu_{i,c}-2r_c\}
&\le a_{i,c}\le\mu_{i,c}.
\label{eq:simultaneous_class}
\end{align}
Consequently, the inequalities remain valid for every candidate in any set
selected adaptively from the calibration statistics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning on $(\mathcal Q,\mathcal I)$, Hoeffding's inequality gives
\[
\Pr\{|\widehat\mu_i-\mu_i|>r\mid\mathcal Q,\mathcal I\}
\le2e^{-2Nr^2}=\frac{\delta}{2M}.
\]
Therefore, a union bound over $M$ candidates spends at most $\delta/2$.
%
Likewise,
\[
\Pr\{|\widehat\mu_{i,c}-\mu_{i,c}|>r_c\mid\mathcal Q,\mathcal I\}
\le2e^{-2N_cr_c^2}=\frac{\delta}{2MC},
\]
so a second union bound over $MC$ pairs spends at most $\delta/2$.
%
The
intersection has probability at least $1-\delta$, and it does not require
different candidates evaluated on the same record to be independent.

On this event, $1-\widehat\mu_i+r$ lies between $R_i$ and $R_i+2r$.
%
Moreover, $\widehat\mu_{i,c}-r_c$ lies between $\mu_{i,c}-2r_c$ and
$\mu_{i,c}$.
%
Applying $[\cdot]_+$ proves the class inequalities.
%
Because
the event covers all $M+MC$ coordinates before selection, taking a
data dependent subset requires no additional union bound over $2^M$ sets.
\end{proof}

The simultaneous event also constrains how calibration screening can change
the best achievable primary coverage.
%
For any pool $\mathcal E$, let $W(\mathcal E)$ be the largest feasible
semantic coverage available from that pool, with value zero if no nonempty set
is feasible.
%
On the event in Theorem~\ref{thm:simultaneous_calibration}, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R^-=\{i:R_i\le\tau_L-2r\},
\qquad
\mathcal R^0=\{i:R_i\le\tau_L\}.
\end{equation}
Then,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R^-\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal R}\subseteq\mathcal R^0,
\qquad
W(\mathcal R^-)\le W(\widehat{\mathcal R})\le W(\mathcal R^0).
\label{eq:coverage_sandwich}
\end{equation}
The left inclusion follows from $R_i^+\le R_i+2r$, whereas the right inclusion
follows from $R_i\le R_i^+$.
%
Hence, calibration preserves the conditional calibration mixture primary value
whenever a primary optimal witness over $\mathcal R^0$ lies entirely in
$\mathcal R^-$.

With $\tau_L=1$, the upper confidence gate is a finite sample admissibility
screen over normalized Brier risk.
%
We therefore refer to $\widehat{\mathcal R}$ as the calibration eligible pool.

\subsection{Exact Primary Coverage under a Hard Budget}

Once calibration fixes the eligible pool, the remaining primary challenge is
combinatorial: the budget may prevent simultaneous coverage of all classes.
%
We characterize this problem and the exact parameterized solver used by
\Fname.

\begin{theorem}[Primary hardness and exact parameterized solver]
\label{thm:primary_solver}
Computing $W^\star$ is NP hard, even when every candidate is eligible, every
cost is one, and all class weights are uniform.
%
Nevertheless, the bitmask
dynamic program returns a
nonempty feasible set $S_0$ with
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S_0)=W^\star.
\end{equation}
It stores at most $2^C$ masks and examines at most
$|\widehat{\mathcal R}|2^C$ state transitions.
%
Thus, its primary stage is
fixed parameter tractable in $C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For hardness, take a Maximum Coverage instance with universe
$\{1,\ldots,C\}$, subsets $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_M$, and cardinality
limit $k$.
%
Set $b_i=1$, $B=k$, and $w_c=1/C$.
%
Maximizing
$C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ then solves that instance up to the constant factor
$1/C$.

For exactness, after processing the first $t$ eligible candidates, associate
each reachable union mask $h$ with its least cost witness.
%
Omitting candidate
$t$ preserves an earlier state, whereas including it extends an earlier
mask to $h\vee\Gamma_t$.
%
Every subset makes exactly one of these choices.
%
Moreover, a lower cost witness for the same mask dominates every higher cost
witness under all future union operations.
%
Induction therefore shows that
the final table contains the least cost witness for every reachable mask.
%
Scanning the budget feasible masks returns exactly $W^\star$.

A $C$-bit mask has at most $2^C$ values, and each eligible candidate extends
each current mask at most once.
%
When all feasible masks have value zero,
the solver returns the least cost feasible singleton, which enforces
the nonempty stacker input without changing the primary value.
\end{proof}

The transition count excludes witness reconstruction and sorting overhead.
%
We restrict the evaluated solver to at most $10^6$ states, and the exactness
guarantee applies to every instance processed within this limit.

\subsection{Secondary Objective and Local Refinement}

Exact primary coverage restricts the comparison to feasible sets that attain
$W^\star$, but it does not make the secondary problem globally tractable.
%
We first explain why the two objectives cannot be replaced by one universal
weighted sum and then state the guarantee of the deployed local refinement.

Let $\mathcal V=\{C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S):S\in\mathcal F_B\}$ contain the
achievable primary values.
%
When $\mathcal V$ has at least two values, let $\Delta_{\mathrm{inst}}$ be its
smallest positive gap.
%
Because $0\le\Phi(S)\le1$, any coefficient
$K>1/\Delta_{\mathrm{inst}}$ makes every maximizer of
$KC_{\mathrm{sem}}+\Phi$ lexicographic for that fixed instance.
%
Normalized class weights can make the smallest positive coverage gap
arbitrarily small, so no universal finite coefficient is available.
%
The strict order in Eq.~\ref{eq:lexicographic_target} avoids this
instance-specific scale tuning.

\begin{proposition}[Secondary structure]
\label{prop:secondary_structure}
For fixed calibration statistics, $U$, $C_{\mathrm{pred}}$, and
$D_{\mathrm{rep}}$ are normalized, monotone, submodular set functions.
%
Consequently, their nonnegative weighted combination $\Phi$ has the same
properties.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $U$ and $C_{\mathrm{pred}}$, each summand applies the nondecreasing
concave function $h_\tau$ to a nonnegative modular sum.
%
Therefore, the gain from adding the same candidate cannot increase as the
current set grows.
%
For
$D_{\mathrm{rep}}$, target $k$ contributes
$\max_{i\in S}\kappa_{i,k}$, whose marginal gain is
\[
\max\{0,\kappa_{a,k}-\max_{i\in S}\kappa_{i,k}\}.
\]
As $S$ grows, this gain also cannot increase.
%
Averaging and taking a
nonnegative weighted sum preserve all three properties.
\end{proof}

Submodularity provides useful structure for $\Phi$, but it does not yield a
global secondary approximation guarantee under the exact primary constraint.
%
The equal primary family
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F^\star=
\{S\in\mathcal F_B:C_{\mathrm{sem}}(S)=W^\star\}
\end{equation}
is not downward closed and need not satisfy an exchange axiom.
%
Standard monotone submodular knapsack guarantees, including $1-1/e$, therefore
do not apply to the secondary refinement stage.

For $S\in\mathcal F^\star$, let $\mathcal N_1(S)$ contain every set obtained
by removing exactly one selected candidate and adding exactly one unselected
candidate while preserving budget feasibility and $W^\star$.

\begin{theorem}[Coverage preservation and one swap certificate]
\label{thm:local_refinement}
Given an exact primary solution, the refinement procedure
terminates after finitely many accepted operations and returns
$\widehat S\in\mathcal F^\star$ such that
\begin{equation}
\Phi(S')\le\Phi(\widehat S)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loc}},
\qquad
\forall S'\in\mathcal N_1(\widehat S).
\label{eq:one_swap_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The constrained seed has coverage $W^\star$.
%
Because
$C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ is monotone and $W^\star$ is already maximal, every
feasible fill keeps that value.
%
The algorithm checks every
accepted replacement for budget feasibility and equality to $W^\star$.
%
Each accepted operation also increases $\Phi$ by more than
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{loc}}$.
%
Because the candidate pool has only finitely many subsets, the procedure
terminates.
%
At termination, the exhaustive
single swap search has found no feasible coverage neutral replacement
whose gain exceeds the tolerance, which proves
Eq.~\ref{eq:one_swap_certificate}.
\end{proof}

The certificate is deliberately local because the refinement procedure
explores only one swap neighbors.
%
During fill, the procedure ranks candidates by gain per byte and stops when
the chosen candidate's absolute gain does not exceed the tolerance.
%
The guarantee does not cover broader additions, multi candidate exchanges, or
the globally optimal secondary value.

\subsection{Effective Coverage under Client Mixture Shift}

Exact primary coverage preserves declared source support, but declared support
alone does not certify current competence.
%
We now connect these quantities for the pretrained returned set and quantify how
client mixture shift changes that connection.

The certificate reuses statistics already computed during calibration and
does not alter the selection objective.

Let $\vartheta_c\in[0,1]$ be a prespecified current competence threshold.
%
To test whether declared source support is backed by current scores, define
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{cert}}(S;\boldsymbol\vartheta)=
\sum_{c=1}^{C}w_c
\mathbf 1\{\exists i\in S:
\Gamma_{i,c}=1\ \wedge\ a_{i,c}\ge\vartheta_c\}.
\label{eq:certified_coverage}
\end{equation}
Under this rule, a class contributes only when a selected detector provides
both declared source support and a conservative current utility score.
%
For comparison, let $C_\mu^a(S;\boldsymbol\vartheta)$ replace
$a_{i,c}\ge\vartheta_c$ by $\mu_{i,c}^a\ge\vartheta_c$, where
$a\in\{\mathrm{cal},\mathrm{dep}\}$.

Let $h=(j,c)$ index a client--class cell.
%
Suppose that detector behavior within each cell remains stable between
calibration and deployment while only the mixture weights change.
%
Let $R_{i,h}\in[0,1]$ denote the cell risk, and let
$\pi_h^{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $\pi_h^{\mathrm{dep}}$ denote the two mixtures.
%
For $a\in\{\mathrm{cal},\mathrm{dep}\}$, define
\begin{equation}
R_i^a=\sum_{j,c}\pi_{j,c}^a R_{i,j,c},
\qquad
\mu_{i,c}^a=\sum_j\pi_{j\mid c}^a(1-R_{i,j,c}),
\label{eq:mixture_quantities}
\end{equation}
and let $\operatorname{TV}(p,q)=\tfrac12\lVert p-q\rVert_1$.
%
Under the calibration design, the means in
Eqs.~\ref{eq:heterogeneous_overall_mean}--\ref{eq:heterogeneous_class_mean}
equal $1-R_i^{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $\mu_{i,c}^{\mathrm{cal}}$, respectively.

\begin{theorem}[Effective coverage under mixture shift]
\label{thm:post_selection_certificates}
Suppose that the within class client mixtures differ by at most $\eta_c$.
%
On the simultaneous calibration event, every adaptively selected set
$\widehat S$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_\mu^{\mathrm{cal}}(\widehat S;
\boldsymbol\vartheta+\boldsymbol\eta+2\boldsymbol r)
&\le C_{\mathrm{cert}}(\widehat S;
\boldsymbol\vartheta+\boldsymbol\eta)
\\
&\le C_\mu^{\mathrm{dep}}(\widehat S;\boldsymbol\vartheta)
\le C_{\mathrm{sem}}(\widehat S).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:certified_coverage_sandwich}
\end{equation}
where $(\boldsymbol r)_c=r_c$ and
$(\boldsymbol\eta)_c=\eta_c$.

Moreover, if
$\operatorname{TV}(\pi^{\mathrm{dep}},\pi^{\mathrm{cal}})\le\eta$, then
\begin{equation}
R_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\le R_i^{\mathrm{cal}}+\eta.
\label{eq:risk_mixture_shift}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:simultaneous_calibration} and the mixture assumption give
\[
\mu_{i,c}^{\mathrm{cal}}\ge\vartheta_c+\eta_c+2r_c
\Longrightarrow a_{i,c}\ge\vartheta_c+\eta_c
\Longrightarrow\mu_{i,c}^{\mathrm{dep}}\ge\vartheta_c.
\]
Intersecting these implications with the fixed support edge
$\Gamma_{i,c}=1$, taking the existential quantifier over selected
candidates, and summing class weights proves
Eq.~\ref{eq:certified_coverage_sandwich}.

For the final claim, total variation bounds the change in the expectation of
any $[0,1]$-valued function.
%
Applying this bound to the cell risks yields
Eq.~\ref{eq:risk_mixture_shift}.
\end{proof}

By requiring both declared source side support and current class conditional
Brier utility, $C_{\mathrm{cert}}$ connects the two under client mixture shift.
%
During selection, \Fname optimizes $C_{\mathrm{sem}}$ and locally refines
$\Phi$ while preserving the exact primary value.
%
Only after selection do $C_{\mathrm{cert}}$ and the mixture sensitivity bound
analyze the pretrained returned set.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate whether \Fname can reduce the cost of integrating pretrained
detectors while preserving the source side class support needed to represent
infrequent attacks.
%
We organize the evaluation around five questions:
\begin{packeditemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1: Detection quality under a budget.}
    %
    How much predictive
    quality and source support does the shared detector retain under a hard
    selection cost budget?
    %
    \item \textbf{RQ2: Client label heterogeneity.}
    %
    How do different client
    label distributions affect partition feasibility, class support, and
    detection quality?
    %
    \item \textbf{RQ3: Selection signals and learners.}
    %
    Which selection
    signals, semantic settings, and learning backends materially change the
    result?
    %
    \item \textbf{RQ4: Score randomization.}
    %
    What detection and attack leakage
    costs accompany stronger Laplace score randomization?
    %
    \item \textbf{RQ5: Scalability and runtime.}
    %
    Does exact selection remain

    deployment scale?
\end{packeditemize}

After this split, the coordinator holds the 10\% selection validation
partition.
%
We keep the test set sealed until both selection and stacker fitting have
finished.
%
Unless stated otherwise, we use five repeated splits,
LightGBM~\cite{lightgbm} for local detectors and the shared stacker, a 50\%
selection cost budget, minimum source support $n_{\min}=2$, class weights that
are inversely proportional to the square root of class frequency, and equal
weights for the three secondary selection signals.

\bheading{Baselines and reference point} We compare \Fname with two baselines
under the same selection cost budget.
%
For the random baseline, we generate a random candidate order from each of 20
fixed selection seeds within a data split.
%
The coordinator scans each order once and adds a detector whenever it fits the
remaining budget, after which we average the 20 selections within the split.
%
Under the individual utility baseline, the coordinator ranks detectors by mean
calibrated Brier utility per selection cost byte and greedily packs the fixed
order.
%
Random selection tests whether an arbitrary budget feasible subset is
sufficient, whereas individual utility ranking tests whether prioritizing
current average behavior sacrifices source side class support.
%
We use the full pool of 66 detectors as the unconstrained reference and the
100\% selection cost reference point.
%
By contrast, \Fname first maximizes weighted source class support and then
refines current predictive quality without reducing that support.
%
We later remove only the source support priority while retaining the same
secondary search.

\bheading{Metrics and statistics} We choose macro F1 as the main detection
score because it gives every class equal weight.
%
In imbalanced security traffic, this prevents a frequent class from hiding
weak detection of a rarer attack class.
%
We also report accuracy for overall correctness, balanced accuracy for average
class recall, and minimum evaluated class recall.
%
Among labels that appear in the sealed test set and belong to the fixed
training class set, the last metric takes the lowest recall.
%
We measure weighted class coverage separately because it describes retained
training support rather than classifier quality.
%
For the DDoS privacy experiment, we report \emph{attack leakage}, which is the
fraction of predictions labeled benign that are actually attacks.
%
We define the selection cost in Eq.~\ref{eq:candidate_cost} and the budget in
Eq.~\ref{eq:logical_budget}.
%
For system results, we add the initial candidate upload and stacker label
upload according to Eq.~\ref{eq:total_logical_communication}.
%
We report means and 95\% Student \(t\) confidence intervals over five repeated
splits unless stated otherwise.
%
When two conditions share a split, we pair their observations.
%
We apply Holm correction within each comparison family that we fixed before
analysis.
%
We recompute every detection score from the reserved test confusion matrix.

\bheading{Experimental environment} We run a centralized simulation on one
machine under Linux and Python~3.9.25.
%
In this simulation, we use NumPy~1.26.4, scikit-learn~1.3.2,
LightGBM~4.6.0, and XGBoost~\cite{xgboost}~1.4.2.
%
We record wall clock time and the logical bytes exchanged by the evaluated
protocol.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}

In summary, \Fname enables post-training integration of authorized pretrained intrusion detectors when historical source records are no longer accessible.
It aligns heterogeneous detector outputs, preserves the best available weighted source class coverage under a deployment budget, and then improves current predictive behavior without sacrificing that coverage.
Across four cybersecurity datasets, \Fname approximately halves logical communication under a 50\% selection-cost budget while maintaining detection performance close to the full detector pool on most tasks.

\noindent\textbf{Coverage Priority and Optimization Guarantees.}
The lexicographic objective is appropriate when preserving source class coverage is a deployment requirement rather than a quantity that can be freely traded for average predictive quality.
By optimizing weighted coverage before current predictive behavior, it prevents secondary gains from compensating for losses in priority classes without requiring instance-specific weighting coefficients.
Importantly, source coverage indicates which attack classes were represented during training, but does not guarantee current competence after distribution shift; coverage and class-specific predictive utility should therefore be examined separately.
Within the calibrated candidate pool, the dynamic program exactly solves the primary coverage problem, whereas the subsequent additions and replacements preserve this optimum but provide only local, rather than global, guarantees for the secondary objective.
The calibration guarantees also rely on the stated mixture-shift assumptions, and deployment under larger distribution shifts requires renewed validation.

\noindent\textbf{Deployment Benefits and Practical Limits.}
The reported communication savings measure logical bytes for model delivery and aligned score uploads, rather than distributed latency or protocol overhead.
Under the 50\% selection-cost budget, \Fname achieves maximum weighted source coverage and near-full-pool predictive quality on MalDroid, Darknet, and DoH, while the larger gap on DDoS shows that maximum source coverage does not necessarily imply full-pool predictive parity.
The mixed effects of secondary signals across datasets further suggest that their benefit is task dependent rather than universal.
Moreover, the observed runtime increase as the attack vocabulary grows identifies the number of classes as an important scaling factor for the exact coverage solver.

\noindent\textbf{Privacy Scope and Utility Tradeoff.}
When enabled, score protection requires each client to perturb every selected score block with fresh Laplace noise before transmission.
For a public record label, each perturbed block satisfies record-level, label-conditional local differential privacy, with privacy loss composing across multiple released blocks.
This guarantee applies only to randomized score uploads and does not protect public labels or contributed detector parameters; likewise, the absence of historical records alone does not constitute a privacy guarantee.
Experiments further show that stronger perturbation can reduce predictive utility and increase attack leakage, indicating that deployment should balance privacy protection against both aggregate detection quality and security-critical class performance.
